# Chapter 3 - Pricing: The Commodity Problem

**Source:** Chapter 3, PDF pages 39-48.

## Job of this chapter
Move the buying decision from **price-driven** to **value-driven**.

## Growth frame
The book reduces growth to:
- getting more customers,
- increasing how much each customer is worth per purchase,
- getting customers to purchase more times.

## Commodity test
A buyer has commoditized the offer when they see alternatives as essentially equal and choose mostly on price.

## Grand Slam direction
Differentiate the offer enough that it becomes difficult to compare directly. The goal is not "slightly better for slightly less." The goal is a different value proposition.

## Use this chapter when
- Competitors keep undercutting price.
- The user says customers ask, "Why are you more expensive?"
- The offer is a retainer, subscription, package, course, or service that looks interchangeable.

## Decision rules
1. List the three closest alternatives.
2. Write why a rational buyer would see them as the same.
3. Change the offer structure, outcome, risk, delivery, or bundle until the comparison becomes less direct.
4. Preserve healthy margin; do not use lower price as the default differentiator.
5. Judge offer economics by gross profit / customer value, not revenue alone.

## Diagnostic questions
- What is the customer really buying: labor, access, result, speed, certainty, convenience, status, or a combination?
- Which piece of the offer makes it impossible to say "this is the same as competitor X"?
- Does the offer produce a value-based decision or a price-based decision?
