# $100M Offers - Decision Cheatsheet

## End-to-end sequence

**Market -> Outcome -> Value -> Problems -> Solutions -> Delivery -> Trim & Stack -> Price -> Scarcity -> Urgency -> Bonuses -> Guarantee -> Name -> Test**

If an upstream step is broken, do not decorate the downstream steps.

## 1. Market first

A workable market should have:
- meaningful pain,
- purchasing power,
- accessible targeting,
- stable or growing demand.

Priority: **Market > Offer > Persuasion**

## 2. Escape commodity pricing

Ask:
- What will the buyer compare this with?
- If three competitors look the same, why would the buyer do anything except pick the cheapest?
- Which part of our outcome, structure, terms, delivery, risk, speed, or access makes direct comparison difficult?

Aim for a **category of one**.

## 3. Widen the price-to-value gap

Do not default to lowering price. Increase value, certainty, specificity, speed, support, convenience, proof, and quality of fulfillment.

## 4. Value Equation

Increase:
- Dream Outcome
- Perceived Likelihood of Achievement

Decrease:
- Time Delay
- Effort & Sacrifice

## 5. Build the core offer

1. Dream Outcome
2. Problems
3. Solutions
4. Delivery vehicles
5. Trim & Stack

For delivery vehicles, vary attention level, customer effort, medium, consumption format, response speed, and 10x / 1/10th assumptions.

Trim in this order:
1. high cost + low value
2. low cost + low value

Keep low cost + high value and high cost + high value when justified.

## 6. Scarcity

Scarcity = **quantity**.

Use only what is real: total capacity, intake capacity per period, cohort seats, limited access, limited units, or limited bonuses.

## 7. Urgency

Urgency = **time**.

Legitimate options: cohort starts, real seasonal promotions, real price/bonus changes, or opportunities whose economics actually decay over time.

## 8. Bonuses

Use bonuses instead of discounting when the core price should remain firm. Each bonus should solve a real obstacle, reduce time/effort/risk, have a benefit-led name, and carry a justified value.

## 9. Guarantees

Guarantee logic: **If X does not happen in Y time, we will Z.**

Choose among unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, or implied/performance-based.

## 10. Naming - M-A-G-I-C

- **M** Magnetic Reason Why
- **A** Avatar
- **G** Goal
- **I** Interval
- **C** Container

Use 3-5 components as needed. Keep names clear and punchy. Generate several and test.

## 11. One-minute offer audit

Find the first important failure:
- Is the market in enough pain?
- Can they pay?
- Can we reach them?
- Is the outcome compelling?
- Do they believe it will work?
- Is it too slow?
- Is it too hard?
- Are important objections unsolved?
- Is fulfillment too costly?
- Is price unsupported by value?
- Is purchase delay the issue?
- Is buyer risk blocking purchase?
- Is relevance unclear?

Fix the earliest/highest-leverage constraint first.
